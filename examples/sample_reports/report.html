How to read this report. Every statistic is compared with the baseline: the distribution of the same statistic between random subsets of the training population (pure sampling noise). That noise is computed under exchangeability inside the reference: any row can stand in for any other, so the two sides of a random split are not systematically distinguishable. The 95th percentile is the distinguishability left by the split alone. The new population exceeds it when the new population is more distinguishable from the reference than those two pieces are from each other. A difference is flagged only if it is statistically significant after multiple-testing correction, larger than the baseline 95th percentile, and at least moderate by effect-size rules of thumb. mild within noise or negligible · moderate real, moderate · severe real, large.

These distances measure whether the two clouds of rows occupy the same region of joint feature space, not one column at a time. They do not depend on trees. Zero: indistinguishable. Larger: the joint distributions differ. Neither can make the verdict severe.

MMD² (radial basis function (RBF) kernel, median-distance bandwidth) compares local neighborhoods: new rows should sit near training rows. Similarity saturates beyond the bandwidth, so it does not grow with how far a shift went. Grey bars are the baseline (random training splits); the dashed line is that baseline's 95th percentile; the orange line is the observed value. The observed value 0.00718 sits to the right of the percentile 2.7e-04, so this distance exceeds sampling noise (permutation p = 0.0099).

Energy distance uses raw Euclidean geometry: a random training–new pair should be about as far apart as a pair from the same group. It reacts more to large location or scale shifts than MMD². Tests

AcronymMeaningReading
KSKolmogorov–SmirnovTwo-sample test of one continuous distribution. Adjusted p below α: the distributions differ. Size is KS D.
ADAnderson–DarlingTwo-sample test with more weight in the tails than KS. Adjusted p below α: the tails differ. Not a severity size.
CvMCramér–von MisesTwo-sample test of the full ECDF gap, not only its maximum. Adjusted p below α: the distributions differ. Not a severity size.
TOSTtwo one-sided testsEquivalence test for two means, margin 0.10 SD. p below α: means agree. Not a test of variance or shape.
TestReading
Chi-squareTest that two samples share one discrete distribution. Levels with expected count below 5 are merged. Adjusted p below α: the category shares differ. Also used on the joint missingness pattern. Size is Cramér's V.
Two-proportion z-testTest that two samples share a rate. Used for missingness, the predicted positive rate, and the share of scores within 0.05 of the threshold. Adjusted p below α: the rates differ. Size is Cohen's h.
Permutation testShuffle the population label and recompute the statistic. p = (1 + shuffles at least as large) / (1 + shuffles). Used for domain-classifier AUC, MMD², energy distance, and the Spearman-matrix Frobenius norm.
Mann–WhitneyTest that domain-classifier scores are higher for new rows than for training rows. For fixed scores this is the same comparison as AUC. The verdict uses the permutation p, because the scores were fitted on these rows.
Fisher zTest that one Spearman correlation differs between the samples. The z variance is 1.06/(n−3) in each sample (Fieller, Hartley, and Pearson 1957). Adjusted p below α: that pair changed.
Binomial testOne-sided test that the new share outside the training 0.5–99.5% range, or the isolation-forest outlier share, exceeds the share expected from the training rule. Adjusted p below α: more new rows fall outside support.
Baseline empirical pShare of random reference splits at least as large as the new population: (1 + such rounds) / (1 + rounds). Beyond baseline means the statistic exceeds that reference's 95th percentile.

KS DKolmogorov–Smirnov statisticLargest vertical gap between the two ECDFs, from 0 to 1. 0.20 means the cumulative shares differ by 20 percentage points at that gap. Distance only: no sign, and it does not separate a mean shift from a spread or tail change. ≥ 0.10 moderate, ≥ 0.20 large, and only with adjusted p below α and a value above the baseline 95th percentile. The same reading applies to score KS D and SHAP KS D.

SMDstandardized mean differenceMean gap divided by pooled SD. Absolute SMD ≥ 0.10 small, ≥ 0.50 large.
JSJensen–ShannonDistance between category shares. 0 means the same frequencies; larger means less overlap.
MMDmaximum mean discrepancyKernel distance between the two joint samples. 0 means the clouds of rows are indistinguishable under the RBF kernel; larger means they occupy different regions of feature space. Local: nearby rows count as similar, and similarity saturates beyond the bandwidth. Cannot be severe.
MMD²squared maximum mean discrepancyUnbiased square of MMD, the stored number. Same reading as MMD: 0 indistinguishable, larger a joint-distribution shift. Cannot be severe.
RBFradial basis functionKernel used for MMD. Bandwidth is the median pairwise distance between rows. Rows closer than the bandwidth count as similar; farther rows do not.

SDstandard deviationSpread of one column. SMD and Wasserstein/SD are in these units.
ECDFempirical cumulative distribution functionShare of rows at or below a value. A sideways gap is a location shift; a steeper curve is a tighter spread.
