What would confirm it

  • About 2,040 labelled new rows (prevalence 45.0%, AUC 0.793) make a 95% AUC interval no wider than ±0.020 (Hanley and McNeil 1982). That resolves one AUC. It does not size a test of the difference from the reference. The result that overturns the headline is an AUC on the worse side of the tolerance.
  • Archive decision.json, metadata.json, checksums of each input, the model file, and the code commit with this page.
  • The model owner decides. A later rerun is the monitoring step. This repository does not schedule one.

What this page leaves out

Marginal tests, kernel tests, dependence tests, support tests, and every mild signal are in report.html. They explain columns that did not earn a cause row. They do not change the headline on this page.

The headline compares the valid performance estimates, and the operating rate when its tolerance was fixed, with those tolerances. The size-matched baseline decides which feature movements are sampling noise. Cause rows are the model-important features that also clear adjusted significance and a moderate effect size.

Appendix

Tests

AcronymMeaningReading
KSKolmogorov–SmirnovTwo-sample test of one continuous distribution. Adjusted p below α: the distributions differ. Size is KS D.
TestReading
Chi-squareTest that two samples share one discrete distribution. Levels with expected count below 5 are merged. Adjusted p below α: the category shares differ. Also used on the joint missingness pattern. Size is Cramér's V.
Two-proportion z-testTest that two samples share a rate. Used for missingness, the predicted positive rate, and the share of scores within 0.05 of the threshold. Adjusted p below α: the rates differ. Size is Cohen's h.
Permutation testShuffle the population label and recompute the statistic. p = (1 + shuffles at least as large) / (1 + shuffles). Used for domain-classifier AUC, MMD², energy distance, and the Spearman-matrix Frobenius norm.
Mann–WhitneyTest that domain-classifier scores are higher for new rows than for training rows. For fixed scores this is the same comparison as AUC. The verdict uses the permutation p, because the scores were fitted on these rows.
Fisher zTest that one Spearman correlation differs between the samples. The z variance is 1.06/(n−3) in each sample (Fieller, Hartley, and Pearson 1957). Adjusted p below α: that pair changed.
Binomial testOne-sided test that the new share outside the training 0.5–99.5% range, or the isolation-forest outlier share, exceeds the share expected from the training rule. Adjusted p below α: more new rows fall outside support.
Baseline empirical pShare of random reference splits at least as large as the new population: (1 + such rounds) / (1 + rounds). Beyond baseline means the statistic exceeds that reference's 95th percentile.

Magnitudes

AcronymMeaningReading
KS DKolmogorov–Smirnov statisticLargest vertical gap between the two ECDFs, from 0 to 1. 0.20 means the cumulative shares differ by 20 percentage points at that gap. Distance only: no sign, and it does not separate a mean shift from a spread or tail change. ≥ 0.10 moderate, ≥ 0.20 large, and only with adjusted p below α and a value above the baseline 95th percentile. The same reading applies to score KS D and SHAP KS D.
PSIPopulation Stability IndexChange in bin shares. Below 0.10 stable, 0.10–0.25 monitor, above 0.25 large.
SMDstandardized mean differenceMean gap divided by pooled SD. Absolute SMD ≥ 0.10 small, ≥ 0.50 large.
ECEexpected calibration errorMean gap between predicted probability and observed rate. Above 0.05, probabilities are off and CBPE is info.
ESSeffective sample sizeKish ratio after weighting. Above 0.50 usable, 0.10–0.50 caution, below 0.10 the weights have collapsed.
ECDFempirical cumulative distribution functionShare of rows at or below a value. A sideways gap is a location shift; a steeper curve is a tighter spread.
StatisticReading
Energy distanceEuclidean distance between the two joint samples. Twice the typical training–new pair distance minus the typical distance inside each group. 0 means indistinguishable; larger means the clouds are farther apart or have different spreads. Reacts more to large location or scale shifts than MMD². Cannot be severe.
